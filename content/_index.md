## Welcome

Join us in Gdansk for a day of talks, workshops, and community — **June 14, 2027**.

The European Seabird Conference 2027 brings together researchers, conservationists, and enthusiasts from across the continent to share the latest findings on seabird ecology, population dynamics, and conservation. Set against the backdrop of the historic city of Gdansk on the Baltic coast, this conference offers a unique opportunity to connect with colleagues and explore one of Europe's most vibrant port cities.

This year's programme spans three days of keynote lectures, parallel sessions, poster presentations, and field excursions. Topics range from long-term monitoring of breeding colonies in the North Atlantic to the effects of climate change on migratory routes in the Mediterranean. Whether you are presenting cutting-edge research or attending for the first time, we warmly welcome you to Gdansk.

## About the Conference

The conference is organised by the Seabird Group in partnership with local ornithological societies and the University of Gdansk. We aim to foster an open and inclusive environment where early-career researchers and established scientists alike can exchange ideas, forge new collaborations, and celebrate the remarkable diversity of seabird life in European waters.

Over the past decade, European seabird populations have faced mounting pressures from overfishing, plastic pollution, offshore energy development, and shifting prey distributions driven by ocean warming. This conference places special emphasis on translating scientific findings into conservation action, with dedicated sessions on policy engagement, marine protected areas, and community-based monitoring programmes.

## Gdansk: A City of History and Nature

Gdansk lies at the mouth of the Vistula River on the southern shore of the Baltic Sea. Long a crossroads of trade and culture, the city boasts a stunning medieval old town, world-class amber museums, and easy access to the Hel Peninsula — one of Poland's finest coastal wildlife areas. Delegates are encouraged to arrive early or stay on after the conference to explore the surrounding landscape, where sandwich terns, little gulls, and common eiders can be found within easy reach of the city centre.

The conference venue, the Gdansk Conference Centre, is located a short walk from the main railway station and offers state-of-the-art facilities for plenary sessions and breakout workshops. A dedicated social programme including a welcome reception, conference dinner, and guided old-town walk will give delegates ample opportunity to enjoy the city's famous hospitality.
